**A public collection of Individualized Education Program (IEP) templates, model forms, and examples from across all 50 U.S. states.**

**About This Project**

During an AI & Behavioral Research Intern, I repeatedly ran into the same problem: it was surprisingly difficult to find examples of how IEPs were structured in different states. Forms, terminology, and available resources varied, and the information was often spread across state education websites, school districts, universities, and other public sources.

I created this repository to bring those materials together in one organized, publicly accessible place.

The goal is to make it easier for students, educators, researchers, developers, and others interested in special education to compare IEP structures and understand how IEP documentation differs across the United States.

**What's Included**

The repository contains publicly available materials representing all 50 U.S. states, including:

Blank IEP templates and forms
State model IEP forms
Sample and completed IEP examples
State Department of Education resources
School district and educational agency examples
Additional public resources when a state does not publish a downloadable blank IEP

Because states use different systems and approaches, the type of example available for each state may vary. Some states publish standardized forms, while others use electronic IEP systems or allow individual school districts to determine the format.

**Why This Repository Exists**

IEPs contain highly structured information about a student's educational needs, goals, accommodations, services, and progress. Yet finding examples from multiple jurisdictions can require searching dozens of separate websites.

This repository is intended to make that research more efficient by creating a centralized 50-state reference collection.

It can be useful for:

Comparing how IEPs are organized across states
Researching differences in forms and terminology
Understanding common components of IEP documentation
Exploring approaches to educational accessibility and special education
Supporting research into tools that could make IEP drafting and review more efficient
Developing and evaluating responsible applications of AI in special education
AI and Future Use

One motivation for creating this collection is to explore how artificial intelligence could eventually help educators and families work more efficiently with complex special-education documentation.

A well-organized collection of public IEP examples can support research into questions such as how AI systems might assist with locating relevant examples, comparing state formats, organizing information, or supporting the drafting process.

This repository itself should be viewed as a reference collection rather than a finished AI training dataset. Any future use for AI development or training should independently consider privacy, licensing, accuracy, bias, and appropriate human oversight.

**Sources and Updates**

The materials in this repository were collected from publicly accessible sources, with preference given to state Departments of Education, public education agencies, school districts, universities, and other educational resources.

IEP forms, policies, and state requirements can change over time. A document in this repository therefore should not be assumed to represent the most current requirements for a particular state.

Where possible, links to original public sources are maintained so users can verify current information.

**Important Note**

This repository is intended for research, educational, and informational purposes. It is not legal advice, special-education advice, or a substitute for current guidance from a school district, state education agency, qualified professional, or applicable law.

The repository is intended to contain only materials that were already publicly available and should not contain private student educational records or personally identifiable student information.

**Project Goal**

The long-term goal is simple:

Make examples of IEP documentation from all 50 states easier to find, compare, study, and use as a foundation for responsible educational research and technology.
